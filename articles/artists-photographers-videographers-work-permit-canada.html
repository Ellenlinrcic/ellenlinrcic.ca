去加拿大表演、拍视频、做演出，需要工作签证吗？

Read in English

最近遇到一个很有意思的问题： 外国艺术家、摄影师或视频制作人员， 如果只是短期到加拿大完成一个项目， 可以拿旅游签证入境吗？ 还是必须申请工作签证？

答案是：不一定。

加拿大对于“工作”的判断， 并不是简单看一个人拿什么签证入境， 也不是只看他的职业， 而是看他来到加拿大以后具体做什么。

有旅游签证，不代表可以工作

这是最容易产生的误解。

旅游签证主要解决的是 “能不能来加拿大”的问题， 并不意味着持有人可以在加拿大随意工作。

但是反过来， 也不是所有在加拿大进行的工作 都必须申请工作签证。

加拿大允许某些外国人在特定情况下， 短期进入加拿大从事工作， 而无需申请工作许可。

艺术、表演、新闻和部分影视制作人员， 就可能属于这种情况。

同样是“艺术家”，结果可能完全不同

例如，一名外国音乐家来温哥华参加两场演出， 演出结束后马上离境。

这类短期表演在符合条件的情况下， 可以不申请工作许可。

一名外国画家来多伦多参加自己的画展开幕式， 与画廊见面、推广已经完成的作品， 也可能只是正常的短期商务活动。

但是，如果一家加拿大酒店聘请一名外国画家 来加拿大住三个月， 专门为酒店绘制大型壁画， 情况就完全不同了。

虽然三个人都可以被称为“艺术家”， 但他们在加拿大从事的活动性质并不一样。

拍视频要看“为谁拍”

影视和视频制作也是如此。

例如，一个海外制作团队来加拿大取景， 为外国公司制作一个短期商业项目， 在符合条件的情况下， 团队成员可能无需申请工作许可。

但是，如果一家加拿大房地产公司 从海外请来一名摄影师， 在温哥华专门为本地楼盘拍摄宣传视频， 那么就需要更加谨慎。

因为这个人已经很可能是在 直接为加拿大企业提供服务。

所以，判断一个摄影师或 videographer 是否需要工作许可， 不能只问： “他是不是来加拿大拍几天就走？”

更重要的是问： “他到底是为谁拍？”

我们通常会先问这几个问题

遇到艺术家、摄影师、摄像师 或影视制作团队的案件， 我们通常首先了解：

  • 来加拿大具体做什么？
  • 谁邀请他来加拿大？
  • 谁是他的雇主？
  • 谁支付他的报酬？
  • 项目是加拿大项目还是海外项目？
  • 最终客户是谁？
  • 在加拿大停留和工作的时间有多长？

这些问题的答案， 往往比“他的职业是什么”更加重要。

案件策略

这类案件最大的风险， 是把“有旅游签证”和 “可以在加拿大工作”混为一谈。

另一个常见误区， 则是认为： “我只来工作几天，所以不需要工作签证。”

工作时间短， 并不自动代表不需要工作许可。

反过来， 有些活动虽然确实属于工作， 却因为符合加拿大特定的豁免规定， 可以合法地在没有工作许可的情况下进行。

因此，这类案件真正需要解决的不是： “能不能拿旅游签证来加拿大？”

而是： “来到加拿大以后准备做什么， 这项活动是否需要工作许可？”

对于艺术家、摄影师、视频制作人员和表演人员来说， 职业名称只是起点。

工作内容、项目性质、客户所在地和商业关系， 才是真正决定案件方向的关键。

Do Artists, Photographers and Videographers Need a Work Permit to Work in Canada?

A common question is: If a foreign artist, photographer or video production professional is coming to Canada for a short-term project, can they simply enter as a visitor, or do they need a work permit?

The answer is: it depends.

Canada does not determine whether a work permit is required simply based on a person’s occupation or the type of visa they hold. What matters most is what they will actually be doing in Canada.

A Visitor Visa Does Not Automatically Allow You to Work

A visitor visa allows a person to travel to Canada and seek entry as a temporary resident. It does not automatically give them the right to work in Canada.

However, the opposite is also true: not everyone who performs work in Canada needs a work permit.

Certain foreign nationals may perform specific types of short-term work in Canada without obtaining a work permit.

This can include some performing artists, media professionals and foreign production personnel.

Not All “Artists” Are Treated the Same

Consider a few examples.

A foreign musician coming to Vancouver to perform two concerts and then leaving Canada may be able to perform without a work permit if the circumstances meet the applicable requirements.

A foreign painter coming to Toronto to attend the opening of an exhibition, meet with galleries and promote artwork already created outside Canada may also be able to enter without a work permit.

But suppose a Canadian hotel hires a foreign artist to spend three months in Canada painting murals throughout the hotel.

That is a very different situation.

All three individuals may call themselves “artists,” but the nature of their activities in Canada is completely different.

For Photographers and Videographers, Ask: Who Is the Work For?

The same principle applies to photography and video production.

For example, an overseas production team coming to Canada temporarily to film a foreign-funded commercial project may, depending on the circumstances, be able to work without a work permit.

On the other hand, if a Canadian real estate company hires a foreign videographer to come to Vancouver specifically to produce promotional videos for local properties, the situation requires much closer examination.

The videographer may be providing services directly to a Canadian business and participating in the Canadian labour market.

So the important question is not simply:

“How many days will you be filming in Canada?”

A more important question is: “Who are you filming for?”

Questions We Look At

When assessing cases involving artists, photographers, videographers or production crews, some of the key questions include:

  • What exactly will you be doing in Canada?
  • Who invited or hired you?
  • Who is paying you?
  • Is the project Canadian or foreign?
  • Who is the ultimate client?
  • How long will you be working in Canada?

The answers to these questions are often much more important than the person’s job title.

Case Strategy

One of the biggest mistakes in these cases is assuming that having a visitor visa means you are allowed to work in Canada.

Another common misunderstanding is:

“I am only coming to work for a few days, so I do not need a work permit.”

A short assignment does not automatically make the work permit-exempt.

At the same time, certain activities may legally be performed without a work permit even though they are considered work.

The real question, therefore, is not simply: “Can I come to Canada on a visitor visa?”

It is: “What exactly will I be doing in Canada, and does that activity require a work permit?”

For artists, photographers, videographers and production professionals, the job title is only the starting point.

The nature of the project, the client, the source of the work and the business relationship are what ultimately determine the appropriate immigration strategy.

Coming to Canada for a short-term project?

Work permit requirements depend on the actual activity, employer, client, source of payment and nature of the project.
