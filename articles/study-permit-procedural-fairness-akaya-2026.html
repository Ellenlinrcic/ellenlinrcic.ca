没有给解释机会就拒签？联邦法院裁定移民局有些情况下可能违反程序公正

Read in English

最近，加拿大联邦法院在 Akaya v. Canada (2026 FC 546) 一案中撤销了一份学生签证拒签决定。 这起案件提醒我们：当签证官准备根据真实性疑虑拒签申请时， 在某些情况下，申请人应当获得回应机会。

究竟发生了什么？

在这个申请中，申请人的留学资金由哥哥提供。 她提交了资助信、照片以及说明双方关系的材料。 但签证官认为，现有证据不足以证明资助人确实是申请人的哥哥， 因此没有接受关于这笔资金的解释，并最终拒绝了学签申请。

问题在于，IRCC在作出决定前， 并没有通知申请人签证官对兄妹关系产生了疑问， 也没有给予申请人补充亲属关系证明的机会。

联邦法院认为，在这种情况下，程序并不公平， 因此撤销了原来的拒签决定，并要求IRCC重新审理案件。

为什么这个判例重要？

很多申请人认为： 签证官觉得材料不够，就应该通知我补件。

事实并不是这样。 加拿大移民申请实行的是申请人自行举证原则。 一般情况下，申请人需要一次性提交完整材料， 签证官并没有义务提醒申请人补充证据。

但是，本案的问题并不仅仅是“材料够不够”。 法院认为，签证官实际上已经开始怀疑申请人陈述的真实性， 也就是怀疑资助人是否真的与申请人存在兄妹关系。

当拒签理由建立在这种真实性疑虑上， 而申请人事先并不知道签证官存在这样的疑问时， 在某些情况下，程序公正可能要求给予申请人回应和解释的机会。

这是否意味着资金拒签都可以翻案？

事情并不是这么简单。

如果签证官只是认为资金不足、收入证明不充分， 或者银行流水不能支持资金来源， 通常仍属于对证据的评估，未必涉及程序公正。

Akaya一案真正强调的是： 当签证官实际上是在否定申请人所陈述的重要事实， 却没有给予申请人相应的回应机会， 拒签决定可能存在法律问题。

对申请人的启示

这起案件也提醒申请人， 在准备资金材料时，不仅要证明有多少钱， 更要证明：

  • 资金来自哪里；
  • 为什么由该资助人提供；
  • 与资助人的关系如何证明；
  • 所有材料之间是否能够互相印证。

收到拒签后，也不要急着认为只是材料准备不足。

有些案件适合重新申请； 有些案件则可能需要进一步分析， 签证官是否在没有给予解释机会的情况下， 对申请人的真实性作出了不利判断。

不同的情况，下一步策略可能完全不同。

Refused Without a Chance to Respond? Federal Court Clarifies When a Study Permit Refusal May Be Procedurally Unfair

The Federal Court recently set aside a study permit refusal in Akaya v. Canada (2026 FC 546). The case highlights an important principle: when a visa officer intends to refuse an application based on concerns about the credibility or truthfulness of the applicant’s evidence, the applicant may, in certain circumstances, be entitled to an opportunity to respond.

What Happened?

In this case, the applicant’s studies were financially supported by her brother. She submitted a sponsorship letter, photographs, and information explaining their family relationship. However, the visa officer concluded that the evidence was insufficient to establish that the sponsor was in fact the applicant’s brother.

As a result, the officer did not accept the explanation regarding the financial support and refused the study permit application.

The key issue was that IRCC never informed the applicant that her relationship with the sponsor was in doubt, nor was she given an opportunity to provide additional documents proving the family relationship.

The Federal Court found that, in these circumstances, the decision-making process was procedurally unfair. The refusal was therefore set aside and the application was returned to IRCC for reconsideration.

Why Is This Case Important?

Many applicants believe that if a visa officer thinks the evidence is insufficient, IRCC should automatically request additional documents. That is not how the process generally works.

Canadian immigration applications operate on the principle that applicants are responsible for submitting complete supporting evidence at the time of application. In most cases, visa officers are under no obligation to ask applicants to provide missing documents.

The issue in Akaya, however, was not simply whether there was enough evidence. The Court found that the visa officer had effectively questioned the truthfulness of the applicant’s claim—specifically, whether the sponsor was genuinely her brother.

When a refusal is based on this type of credibility concern, and the applicant has no reason to know that such a concern exists, procedural fairness may require that the applicant be given an opportunity to respond before a decision is made.

Does This Mean Every Financial Refusal Can Be Challenged?

No.

If a visa officer simply concludes that the available funds are insufficient, the income evidence is inadequate, or the bank records do not adequately support the source of funds, the decision is generally considered an assessment of the evidence and does not necessarily raise procedural fairness concerns.

What Akaya emphasizes is different. When a visa officer effectively rejects a key factual claim made by the applicant without first giving the applicant an opportunity to address that concern, the refusal may involve a procedural fairness issue.

What Does This Mean for Applicants?

This case serves as an important reminder that financial documentation should do more than demonstrate the amount of available funds.

Applicants should also be prepared to establish:

  • where the funds come from;
  • why the sponsor is providing financial support;
  • how the relationship with the sponsor can be verified; and
  • how all supporting documents work together to support the application.

If a study permit application is refused, it is also important not to assume that the problem is simply insufficient documentation.

Some cases are best addressed through a stronger re-application, while others may require a careful review of whether the refusal was based on credibility concerns without giving the applicant an opportunity to respond.

The appropriate strategy will depend on the specific circumstances of each case.

Concerned about procedural fairness?
