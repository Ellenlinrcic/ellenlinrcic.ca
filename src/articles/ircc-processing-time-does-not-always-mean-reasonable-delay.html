还在 IRCC 预计处理时间内，也可能已经等得太久？——Dai 案给申请人的重要提醒

Read in English

很多加拿大移民申请人在等待时间越来越长之后， 都会去 IRCC 网站查询 Processing Time。

如果系统显示自己的申请仍然处于预计处理范围内， 大多数人的第一反应可能都是： 那就只能继续等。

但是，加拿大联邦法院最近审理的 Dai v. Canada (Citizenship and Immigration), 2026 FC 931 提出了一个很值得关注的问题：

IRCC 自己预计的处理时间， 是否就等于法律意义上的“合理等待时间”？

答案并不一定。

已经等了 42 个月，IRCC 却告诉她：可能还要再等 39 个月

Dai 于 2023 年 1 月通过 Home Child Care Provider Pilot 递交永久居民申请。

到了案件进入联邦法院审理阶段， 她已经等待大约 42 个月， 仍然没有收到最终决定。

但有意思的是， 她并不是简单地“超过了 IRCC 公布的处理时间”。

根据当时 IRCC 提供的 processing time 信息， 她前面仍然有大量申请等待处理， 预计可能还需要大约 39 个月。

换句话说， 从 IRCC 的处理系统来看， 她的申请仍然在等待队伍中。

如果继续按照这个速度处理， 从申请递交到最终决定， 总等待时间甚至可能超过六年。

于是一个非常重要的问题出现了：

既然 IRCC 自己都预计申请人还需要等待 39 个月， 为什么 Federal Court 仍然可以认为目前的延误已经不合理？

IRCC 的 Processing Time 并不是一个法律上的“安全线”

Dai 案最值得关注的地方就在这里。

IRCC 网站公布的 processing time 可以帮助申请人了解目前案件大概需要等待多久， 但它并不意味着：

只要申请仍然处于 IRCC 预计的处理时间内， 所有延误在法律上就一定是合理的。

在 Mandamus 案件中， 法院需要判断的并不仅仅是 申请有没有超过 IRCC 网站上的预计时间。

法院还会进一步考虑：

  • 这个申请为什么需要等待这么久？
  • 政府是否能够对如此长期的等待提供合理解释？

这两个问题， 与单纯查询 Processing Time 并不是一回事。

Dai 案的问题不仅仅是“积压太严重”

Dai 案还有一个非常重要的背景。

Home Child Care Provider Pilot 此前受到 Ministerial Instructions 的管理， 其中包括申请处理数量和相关安排。

但随着 Ministerial Instructions 发生变化， 原有的处理机制被撤销， 而对于已经进入系统、 仍然等待处理的申请， 新的安排并没有提供同样清晰的处理框架。

法院因此特别关注： IRCC 究竟依据什么机制， 让这些申请继续等待如此长的时间？

如果政府只是告诉申请人：

“你的申请仍然在队伍里， 根据目前速度预计还要 39 个月。”

这能够说明现实中可能还需要等待多久， 却不一定能够解释：

为什么这个具体申请合理地需要等待这么久？

最终， Federal Court 认为有关延误没有得到充分解释， 并命令 IRCC 在 60 天内 对 Dai 的永久居民申请作出决定。

这是否意味着 Processing Time 以内也可以去 Federal Court？

不能这样理解。

Dai 案并没有建立一个简单规则：

“即使没有超过 Processing Time， 也可以申请 Mandamus。”

Mandamus 是否成立， 仍然取决于具体案件。

法院会考虑实际等待时间、 申请类别、 案件复杂程度、 延误是否由申请人造成、 政府是否提供了合理解释， 以及申请人是否还有其他有效的救济方式等因素。

Dai 已经等待约 42 个月， 而且按照当时 IRCC 的估算， 还可能继续等待约 39 个月。

再加上这个项目本身特殊的政策和处理背景， 法院最终才认为有理由介入。

所以， 这个案件不能简单套用到 所有等待中的移民申请。

Processing Time 告诉你的，和法院问的，其实是两个问题

这是 Dai 案对普通申请人最有价值的地方。

当我们打开 IRCC 网站查询 Processing Time 时， 我们实际上是在问：

“按照 IRCC 目前的处理速度， 我大概还需要等多久？”

但在 Mandamus 案件中， 法院需要回答的问题更接近：

“政府有没有合理理由， 让这个申请继续等待这么久？”

两者看起来很相似， 却并不是同一个问题。

因此， IRCC 公布的 Processing Time 当然是评估案件延误的重要参考， 但它并不是判断延误是否合理的唯一标准。

对正在等待申请结果的人有什么启示？

如果申请只是正常等待， 或者刚刚超过 IRCC 公布的 Processing Time， 当然不意味着应该马上考虑 Federal Court。

但对于一些已经等待数年、 案件长期没有实质性进展的申请来说， 仅仅因为 IRCC 网站仍然显示需要继续等待， 也不一定意味着申请人只能无限期地等下去。

真正值得了解的是：

  • 案件为什么还没有处理？
  • 长期延误是否有具体而合理的解释？
  • IRCC 是否仍然在按照一个明确的处理机制推进这个申请？

如果申请已经出现异常长期的停滞， 可以进一步通过 Webform、 GCMS notes 或其他方式了解案件状态。

在适当情况下， 也可以咨询加拿大律师， 评估是否存在寻求 Mandamus 救济的可能。

需要说明的是， Mandamus 属于 Federal Court 司法程序。

RCIC 可以协助分析移民申请本身及案件处理情况， 但 Federal Court 诉讼需要由 具备相应执业资格的加拿大律师处理。

写在最后

Dai 案最值得记住的， 可能不是“42 个月”， 也不是“39 个月”。

而是这两个数字放在一起所提出的问题：

一个人已经等了 42 个月， 政府告诉她还要再等 39 个月—— 这是否仅仅因为 IRCC 这样预计， 就当然属于合理的等待？

Federal Court 在 Dai 案中的回答告诉我们： IRCC 的 Processing Time 很重要， 但它并不是判断行政延误是否合理的最终标准。

对于长期等待的申请人来说， 与其只问： “我有没有超过 Processing Time？”

有时候更重要的问题是：

“为什么我的申请需要等这么久？”

Still Within IRCC’s Estimated Processing Time — But Already Waiting Too Long? What Dai v. Canada Tells Applicants

When a Canadian immigration application takes longer than expected, one of the first things applicants usually do is check IRCC’s online processing time.

If the system indicates that the application is still within the estimated processing period, most applicants assume there is little they can do other than continue waiting.

A recent Federal Court decision, Dai v. Canada (Citizenship and Immigration), 2026 FC 931 , raises an interesting question:

Does IRCC’s estimated processing time necessarily determine whether a delay is legally reasonable?

Not necessarily.

She Had Already Waited 42 Months — and IRCC Estimated Another 39 Months

Dai submitted her permanent residence application under the Home Child Care Provider Pilot in January 2023.

By the time the matter reached the Federal Court, approximately 42 months had passed without a final decision.

What makes this case particularly interesting is that this was not simply a situation where the applicant had exceeded IRCC’s published processing time.

Based on the processing information available at the time, IRCC estimated that she could still have approximately 39 more months to wait.

In other words, from the perspective of IRCC’s processing system, her application was still in the queue.

If processing continued at that pace, the total period between submission and a final decision could potentially exceed six years.

This raises an important question:

If IRCC itself estimated that another 39 months of waiting could be expected, why could the Federal Court nevertheless find that the delay had already become unreasonable?

IRCC Processing Times Are Not a Legal “Safe Zone”

This is perhaps the most important point arising from Dai.

IRCC’s published processing times help applicants understand how long applications may currently take.

But they do not necessarily mean that:

Any delay falling within IRCC’s estimated processing period is automatically reasonable as a matter of law.

In a mandamus application, the Court is not limited to asking whether the applicant has exceeded the processing time displayed on IRCC’s website.

A more fundamental question may be:

Why has this particular application taken so long, and has the government adequately justified the delay?

That is different from simply asking how long IRCC currently estimates an application will take.

The Issue in Dai Was More Than Just a Large Backlog

There was another important aspect of the case.

The Home Child Care Provider Pilot had been governed by Ministerial Instructions that affected how applications were processed, including annual intake and processing arrangements.

Those instructions subsequently changed.

The Court examined what happened to applications that remained in the processing inventory following those changes and whether there continued to be a sufficiently clear basis for the lengthy delay.

This distinction matters.

Telling an applicant:

“Based on the current queue, you may have another 39 months to wait”

may provide an estimate of how long the applicant is likely to wait in practice.

But it does not necessarily answer a different question:

Why is it reasonable for this particular application to require such a lengthy period before a decision is made?

Ultimately, the Federal Court was not satisfied that the delay had been adequately justified and ordered IRCC to make a decision on Dai’s permanent residence application within 60 days.

Does This Mean You Can Seek Mandamus While Still Within IRCC’s Processing Time?

Not necessarily.

Dai should not be interpreted as creating a general rule that applicants can seek mandamus simply because they are unhappy with the length of processing, even if they remain within IRCC’s estimated timeline.

Mandamus is a discretionary judicial remedy, and whether a delay is unreasonable depends on the circumstances of the individual case.

Relevant considerations may include:

  • the actual length of the delay;
  • the nature of the immigration program;
  • the complexity of the application;
  • whether the applicant contributed to the delay;
  • the reasons provided by the government; and
  • whether other adequate remedies remain available.

Dai had already waited approximately 42 months and, based on the information available at the time, potentially faced another 39 months of waiting.

The particular processing history and policy framework of the caregiver program were also important to the Court’s analysis.

The decision therefore should not be automatically applied to every immigration application experiencing a long processing time.

IRCC’s Processing Time and the Court’s Question Are Not Exactly the Same

This is perhaps the most useful lesson from Dai for applicants.

When we check IRCC’s processing time, we are essentially asking:

“Based on IRCC’s current processing pace, approximately how much longer might I have to wait?”

In a mandamus case, however, the Court may need to consider a different question:

“Does the government have an adequate justification for requiring this applicant to continue waiting for this length of time?”

The two questions are related, but they are not the same.

IRCC’s published processing time can certainly be relevant when assessing delay, but it is not necessarily the sole measure of whether a delay is reasonable.

What Does This Mean for Applicants Who Have Been Waiting a Long Time?

An application that is proceeding normally— or has only recently exceeded IRCC’s published processing time— does not automatically justify Federal Court proceedings.

However, where an application has remained unresolved for years with little meaningful progress, the fact that IRCC’s website still indicates a lengthy processing period does not necessarily mean that the applicant must simply continue waiting indefinitely.

The more useful questions may be:

  • Why has the application not been finalized?
  • Is there a specific and reasonable explanation for the prolonged delay?
  • Is the application still moving through a clearly defined processing framework?

Applicants experiencing unusually lengthy delays may consider obtaining more information through IRCC Webform inquiries, GCMS notes, or other available channels.

Where the delay becomes exceptional, legal advice may also be appropriate to assess whether mandamus could be available.

It is important to note that mandamus is a Federal Court remedy.

An RCIC may assist in reviewing the underlying immigration application and its processing history, but Federal Court proceedings must be handled by a lawyer authorized to practise before the Court.

Final Thoughts

Perhaps the most striking part of Dai is not the 42 months already waited, or even the 39 months that IRCC estimated might still remain.

It is the question created when those two numbers are considered together:

If an applicant has already waited 42 months and IRCC says another 39 months may still be required, does that waiting period become reasonable simply because it appears in IRCC’s processing estimate?

Dai provides an important reminder that IRCC’s processing time is relevant, but it is not necessarily the final legal measure of whether an administrative delay is reasonable.

For applicants facing an exceptionally long wait, the most important question may therefore not always be:

“Am I outside IRCC’s processing time?”

Sometimes, the better question is:

“Why does my application need to take this long?”

Still within IRCC’s processing time?

IRCC’s estimated processing time is an important reference, but it is not necessarily the only factor when assessing whether an exceptionally long delay is reasonable.

即使申请仍在 IRCC 预计处理时间内， 如果已经出现异常长期的停滞， 仍然需要进一步了解延误原因、 案件是否有实质进展， 以及 IRCC 是否能够提供具体而合理的解释。
